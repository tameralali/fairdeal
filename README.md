# The Aquarium

Private online poker for a home game. It plays like PokerNow (share a link, approve seats, track the ledger), only members you invite can get in, and every deal can be checked by the players themselves.

## What it does

**One club, in dollars and cents.** The site is a single club, The Aquarium, and every account is in it. All amounts are dollars and cents, down to $0.01 (blinds of $0.10/$0.25 work). The home page is the club, kept short. At the top are the logo (a cream fish on a felt-green tile, also the icon in the browser tab) and The Aquarium in the same font as the rest of the site, your balance (tap it for your own ledger entries) and a menu under your name (People and Invite someone for admins, Change your name, Sign out). Under the club's note come the games. Each game is one card: a game being dealt is felt green with a dot that pulses beside Live, and one waiting for players or not started yet is a quieter dark card that says so. Each card says in a sentence what the game plays, its blinds and its buy-in (PLO on two boards, then Hold'em. Blinds 0.25/0.50, any buy-in.), its extras in a fainter line (bomb pots, straddles, the 7-2 game, how many times it can run, when it ends), who is sitting (their faces, then You, Omar, Lina and 2 others, 5 of 8 seats taken), and has one button: Join, Back to the table if you are sitting there, or Watch when it is full. Games being dealt come first. With no game on, the page says so and links the last game. Under the games are the club's balances: every member's balance, credit limit, what they have available and on tables, sorted by balance, the most first, with what members hold and what is on tables now. Admins open a member there to add to or take from their balance, set their credit, settle up or remove them. Members who cannot see everyone see Your money instead: their balance, what they can play with, their credit and what is on a table. Under the balances are the club's history, in tabs: Past games, Leaderboard (the results), Stats, Hands and Ledger (every movement, or a member's own entries, with a download as a spreadsheet at the bottom). The page remembers the tab you were on. The home page, the sign-in page and the hand checker are dark, with the felt's green in the buttons and highlights and a faint glow at the top. Each game opens in its own tab, so the club page stays open in the first one; starting a game opens it in a new tab too. Admins can end a game from the club page (End game under its button) or at the table (End game at the bottom of Options): the hand in progress finishes, then everyone is cashed out to their balance.

**Start a game.** Admins open games from a pop-out that sets the format, stakes and seats, players needed to start, the clock, bomb pots, running it, the rathole time, what happens to folded cards, and seating. It remembers the last game. At the top it says what kind of game it is: a cash game or a tourney.

**Tourney.** Everyone buys in for the same amount, which is their starting stack, up to a set number of times each (1 for no rebuys, or no limit), and buying in (sitting down or rebuying) can close after a chosen level. The blinds go up on a clock: a list of levels, one a line such as 0.25/0.50, filled in with standard steps of about one and a half times the level before (0.25/0.50, 0.50/1, 0.75/1.50, 1/2, 1.50/3, ...) and editable, every 5 to 60 minutes. The clock starts with the first hand, a new level starts with the next hand after its time, never in the middle of one, and the clock stops while the game is paused. The top bar shows the level with the time left on it, the blinds after it and the prize pool. There is no adding chips: a player who loses their last chip can rebuy (the same pop-up as a cash game's, for the one buy-in, with how many buy-ins they have left) or, with none left or buying in closed, is out and leaves their seat, taking the next place from the bottom (several out in one hand are placed by the stacks they started it with). The tourney ends when one player has every chip, or when its time runs out or the host ends it, and those still in are then placed by their stacks. It pays one of two ways, chosen when it is set up. Chips are cash: everyone cashes out the chips they hold, as in a cash game, so the last player standing takes the whole pool. By place: the buy-ins make a prize pool paid by finishing place with a split such as 65/35 or 50/30/20 (with fewer players than paid places, the places there are share it all), and a player who leaves early leaves their chips in the pool. The places and what each was paid show at the table when it is over, and the game's card on the home page is tagged Tourney with its buy-in, its level while it is played and its winner after. A game under way keeps its buy-in and how it pays; the host can still change its levels, their length, the buy-ins per player and the split.

**Presets.** Any setup can be saved as a named preset, shared by every admin. Presets show under Start a game on the home page and at the top of the pop-out: one click fills in everything, and a preset can be updated or deleted from there. A game started from a preset is named for its day, such as Sunday 22 Nov floppies, by the clock of whoever starts it; the name can still be changed before it starts. A name like that kept from the last game moves to the day's date when Start a game opens.

**Players to start.** The first hand waits until a set number of players are in (2 to the table size). After that, play goes on with two or more.

**Rathole time.** A player who stands up and sits back down at the same table within the set time (30 minutes to 4 hours, or the rest of the game) has to bring at least the stack they left with, even above the maximum. The seat form fills that amount in and shows it as the rathole minimum.

**Sitting down and adding chips.** Taking a seat opens a short form: the amount, a Max button that fills in the most you can bring (the table's maximum, or all you have with your credit if that is less), your club balance and credit under it, and two buttons, Sit now or Sit while away (seated but away until I'm back). You sit under your account name. Add chips is the same form. A problem, such as an amount under the minimum, shows when you press a button.

**Rebuying.** When a hand takes your last chip, a pop-up gives you 10 seconds to rebuy, counting down, with the same amount, Max, balance and credit; it sits low on the screen, so the hand that just ended stays in view. The next deal waits for it (the result is on the table for most of that time anyway), and goes on at once when you rebuy or press Not now, or if you go away or leave. A player who is away or not connected is not waited for, and Add chips still works later.

**Format.** No Limit Hold'em, Pot Limit Omaha (4 cards) and PLO5, played as a list of steps that repeats: each step is one game for a number of orbits (1 to 50) or a number of hands (1 to 500), on one board or two. For example 2 orbits of PLO, then 10 hands of hold'em on a double board, then back to PLO. An orbit ends when the button has gone around the table once. Quick picks cover the usual mixes. Up to 10 seats; the seat limit drops when the deck can't deal everyone (9 with PLO5, 8 with PLO5 on two boards).

**Double board.** Two boards are dealt, board 1 then board 2 on each street, and every pot is split between them. It can be set per format step and for bomb pots. A double board hand is run once only, bomb pot or not: the two boards already split the pot two ways, so there is no vote to run it again. (Hand records from before this rule, run twice on two boards, still replay and check.)

**Bomb pots.** Every N hands, a random percentage of hands, both (at least every N hands, and the chance can bring one sooner), or called by the host for the next hand. The bomb pot size is set in big blinds, a bomb pot can be played as a different game from the one running, and it can be dealt on two boards. Everyone puts the size in, there is no preflop betting, and the flop comes straight away. Before it is dealt, a lit bomb drops onto the felt and goes off in a comic BOMB POT burst, with a fizz and a bang when the table's sound is on; the cards come after it, and the first player's clock waits the two seconds it takes. Random bomb pots are decided from the hand's sealed seed, so players can check they were not picked by hand. The size is the only ante there is: other hands have none, and with bomb pots off the size, game and board fields are hidden and nothing about them has to be filled in.

**Run it once to four times.** When everyone is all in before the river (preflop, on the flop or on the turn), the players in the hand pick how many times to run it, before any cards are turned over. Picks are open: each player's pick shows on their seat for the whole table and on everyone's vote buttons (the number someone else picked carries their name, and numbers nobody picked are dimmed), and anyone can change theirs while the vote is open. If everyone picks the same number, it runs that many times straight away. A pick of once settles it at once. If everyone has picked but the numbers differ, there are 15 seconds to settle on one; with no agreement by then, or after 45 seconds in all, it runs once, so a big pot has time to be talked over. A player who has left or lost their connection is not waited for: the others decide between them, though a pick made before someone dropped still counts. Look and feel can pick for you: always once, or go with whatever number the others pick. The host sets the most runs allowed (2, 3 or 4). Every pot, side pots included, is split across the runs. On the table the cards stay full size: the cards the runs share are drawn once, and each run's own cards stack in their column, each later run over the one before with every card's number and suit still showing. Beside each run's row (in a list under the board on a phone) are each player's odds of winning that run while its cards are still to come, then its winner and their hand as soon as its last card is down; each player's odds for the run being dealt also sit on their seat, as for a hand run once. When a run's last card is down, its winner's odds count up to 100% and the others' down to 0%, and that stays up for a moment before the next run's odds. An all-in runout takes its time: 2.9 seconds before its first card, 2.95 seconds between streets, and 1 second more before each further run starts. Extra runs come off the same sealed deck, so in very full PLO5 pots the deck can limit how many runs are possible. A river all-in has nothing left to deal, so there is no vote.

**Host controls.** Approve or decline seat requests and rebuys (or approve automatically), set a player to sitting out, remove a player, adjust stacks, pause after the current hand, end the game, and change any setting between hands. Every change is written to the table log.

**Time.** Seconds per decision, time banks per player (3 by default) and a game length. Each time bank is 30 more seconds: on your turn Extra time (or T) uses one, it shows how many you have left, and you can use more than one in a hand. Each player gets one back every so many hands dealt to them (15 by default; 0 for never), up to the number they started with; setting fewer during a game cuts everyone down to the new number. Games and presets saved with the old seconds of time bank get 3 time banks, or none if theirs was 0. When the table's sound is on, a high beep sounds halfway through your clock and a quick double beep when a fifth of it is left (and both again on the time a time bank adds). When the seconds to act run out, you are checked or folded. A table that deals on its own starts the clock with its first hand. When the game length runs out, the hand in progress finishes and everyone is cashed out. The host can add time or remove the limit. Players who run out of time are checked or folded and marked Away until they click "I'm back". Pressing Away yourself in the middle of a hand folds you out of it when it comes to you (at once if it is your turn, even when you could check), and you sit out from the next hand. Everyone sees a red Away in the place of the away player's cards, with how long they have been away (while no hand is being dealt, before the first one or with too few players, everyone else seated shows a blue Waiting there instead, with how long they have waited), and they get a big I'm back in the place of the action buttons (on top of the show-your-cards panel when that is up). After a hand that reached the flop, folded there or shown down, the result stays up for 9 seconds before the next deal (a little longer when it ran more than once); a hand everyone folded before the flop moves on after 3 seconds, or 9 when its winner holds 7-2 in a 7-2 game, to show it; when no next hand can be dealt then (too few players, someone away, the game paused), the felt clears and shows the table as it is: PokerNow's slowest showdown setting, time to show cards. The host can also start a clock in the middle of a game (30 minutes, 1, 1½ or 2 hours, from Clock in the top bar or Options, Time), then add time or remove it; clicking the countdown in the top bar opens the same controls.

**In-game ledger.** Buy-ins, cash-outs, current stack, net, hands played, VPIP, PFR and aggression for everyone who played, and CSV download. Pick a player for their sheet: money, every stat, and their biggest wins and losses, each one a click from the replayer.

**Live stats.** VPIP, PFR, 3-bet, fold to 3-bet, ATS (attempt to steal), fold to steal, AF and AFq (aggression), c-bet, fold to c-bet, WTSD, W$SD, WWSF, net and bb/100, updated after every hand. They can be shown for this game or all games in the club, for every game type or one of them. Each seat shows VPIP / PFR / AF and the hands behind them for the game being played: the HUD. The HUD button (top right, beside the sound button; in Menu on a phone) opens a panel to turn it off or on, take its numbers from this game or all games in the club, and switch it on or off for each player at the table, with each player's numbers beside their name; a player's sheet has the same switch. It only changes what you see. Bomb pots have no preflop betting, so they only count toward the numbers after the flop. Members see how everyone plays; other members' results follow the club's ledger setting.

**Hand history and replayer.** Hands opens one screen: every hand of the game in a list on the left, and the replayer on the right. Player picks whose hands to list: Everyone, or one player, which lists only the hands they were dealt into, with their result in each. The list sorts by newest, oldest, biggest pots, biggest wins or biggest losses (for one player, theirs; for everyone, the biggest single win or loss in each hand), can show only showdowns, all-ins or bomb pots, and can be narrowed to one game; each row has the hand number, the chosen player's result for that hand in green or red (for everyone, the pot and who won the most), their cards and the time, and the total for the hands listed sits at the top. The replayer goes step by step: blinds, each action, each street as it was dealt (when a hand is run more than once, each run goes flop, turn, river in turn) and the result, with a bar of steps marked F, T and R to jump to. Stepping past the end of a hand carries on into the next hand of the list, Play does the same after a short pause on the result, and the buttons either side go straight to the previous or next hand (arrow keys: left and right step, up and down change hands, space plays). Details shows the full record and this browser's deck check for the same hand. A finished game opens on its hand history. On a phone the replay fills the screen and Hands brings up the list. Open in new tab (at the top) carries the history on in a tab of its own at the same hand, step, player and sort, with no seat, sounds or chat, and closes it at the table; that tab keeps up with new hands while the game runs, and Go to the table goes back. A game's page opens its replays the same way. The replayer shows cards the way the hand history does: your own, cards shown at showdown, and every card once that hand's seed is revealed.

**Every hand of every game.** The Hands tab on the club page lists the hands of all the games together: pick a session (one game, or every session), a player (Everyone, You or anyone who played), the order (newest, oldest, biggest pots, biggest wins, biggest losses), what to show (all hands, showdowns, all-ins, bomb pots, two boards, straddled pots, 7-2 paid), how many times a hand was run (any, more than once, once, twice and so on, from what was played) and the game. Each row has the game and hand number, when it was played, what it was (PLO, two boards, ran 3 times, all-in), the cards that can be seen, and the result: the chosen player's, or the pot and who won it, or for biggest wins and losses the biggest single one and whose it was. The count of hands sits at the top with the chosen player's total over them (your own, or anyone's for those who see the whole club). A hand opens the replayer over the page, with the same list beside it, and stepping or playing past the end of a hand goes on into the next hand of the list, whichever game it is from; each hand is drawn with its own game's felt and checked as that game's. Open in new tab carries it on at /hands, a tab of its own with the same hand, step and choices, which keeps up with new hands while games are played. Everyone gets the cards they would see in each game's own hand history: their own, shown ones (only the cards shown, for a partial show), and every card once the game has opened its deck. Amounts are in dollars and cents across every game.

**Straddle.** With Allow a straddle on (Options or Start a game), each player can turn on Straddle in the bar at the bottom: whenever they are under the gun they post twice the big blind before the deal, a live blind, so action starts after them and they act last before the flop with the option to raise. Heads-up the player under the gun is the button, who has posted the small blind: they put in the rest of twice the big blind (a button straddle), and the big blind acts first. Not in bomb pots, and only with more than the straddle behind. Their seat shows Straddle and the replayer says who straddled.

**The 7-2 game.** Set a bounty (Options or Start a game) and a Hold'em hand won, all of the pot or part of it, holding a 7 and a 2 of any suits, with both cards shown, collects that bounty from every other player dealt in, up to what each has. Won at a showdown, it is paid at once; won when everyone folded, it is paid the moment the winner shows both cards (the bar reminds them). Two winners holding 7-2 collect nothing. The bounty is announced on the table, logged, kept with the hand, and counted in every result and stat. The top bar shows the bounty while the game is on.

**A game's page.** A finished game opens its own page from the club's games and from Results: who won the game, each player's net, buy-in, what they took home, hands played and pots won, highlights (biggest pot, biggest win and loss, best hand shown, most pots won, 7-2 bounties, hands run more than once), the biggest hands largest pot first (each opens the replayer on that hand), and the game's stats. A game still going shows the same page with the numbers so far.

**Settling up.** Settle on a member asks who they paid, or who paid them: pick that player and the amount (it suggests the smaller of the two balances) and one settlement is written as a pair of entries, plus for one player and minus for the other, each naming the other and how it was paid. Settling with the club in cash, which sets the balance to zero, is still there.

**Results.** The Leaderboard tab on the club page adds up every game: each player's net, games played and won, hands, best and worst game and when they last played, biggest winner first. Results and stats both take a period: all time, the last 7, 30 or 90 days, this year, or any two dates. Other members' results follow the same rule as the ledger: admins see them, members only when the club settings allow it.

**Clearing history.** The owner can clear everything played from Club settings, after typing CLEAR: every game with its hands and log, the club ledger and all stats go, and every balance is set to zero. Accounts, roles, credit limits, presets, invites and the join link stay. Anyone at a game is told it was removed.

**Club ledger.** Members hold a balance across games. Admins add or take money with a note, set a credit limit per member (how far below zero they may buy in), record settlements, and choose whether members can see everyone's balance and send money to each other. Tables seat members straight from their balance and pay back into it when they stand up, so games run without an admin present. Every movement is a ledger entry with the balance after it, downloadable as CSV.

**The table.** A plain green felt in a near-black room, drawn in Apple's manner: Apple's own font (SF Pro on Apple devices, Inter elsewhere), frosted panels with hairline edges, rounded corners, Apple's colours, and words in sentence case. Each player is a frosted dark box with their name and stack, their cards fanned over its left end. Cards are drawn like PokerNow's: white, with a heavy serif number (Abril Fatface in bold) and a big suit low on the right, in four colours by default (PokerNow's blue for diamonds, green for clubs); in a hand a small suit sits under the number. Amounts on the table, in its log and in the hand history have no dollar sign (the club pages keep it). Cards are dealt one at a time: one to each player, starting left of the button, then the next round, each coming from the middle of the table, and board cards are turned over one after another, with short pauses between streets. Bets sit in front of each player on the felt, never over anyone's box, cards or labels, the pot, the board or another bet (a long amount that would reach over something moves to the nearest clear place), the pot is in the middle with the total including bets above it, and the dealer button rides on the dealer's box. A player's last action (Call, Raise, +8.00) sits just outside their box, so it covers none of their name, stack or numbers, and it keeps clear of the game, blinds and tags along the top. Folded players show an X. Free seats are dashed boxes with the seat number in the corner, as on PokerNow, with SIT in the middle for someone who can take one; once you are seated they are not drawn at all. No box, name or card ever covers the board: on a computer, seats keep above or below it where there is room, as on PokerNow, and wherever a box, its cards or a bet would still reach over it, the middle of the table is drawn just small enough to keep clear (checked for every table size, from heads-up to ten, on one board or two). On a phone the seats at the sides keep clear of two boards one above the other, and of the list of runouts under the board, and after a hand on two boards one line says who won (each board names its winner beside it). The board stays put when the results come in under it. When everyone left is all in with cards face up, each hand shows its chance of winning, worked out in the browser from the cards on the table, in green above 50% and red below (grey at an even 50%); the numbers move to their new value within a quarter of a second of each card, and when the last card is down the winner's number counts up to 100% and the rest count down to 0% in half a second. While the flop is out, the odds for every possible turn are worked out with it, so the turn's numbers are there the moment it lands; when it runs more than once the odds are given for each runout beside it instead (folded cards count as unknown, so nobody learns anything they could not already see). Shown hands and your own hand are labelled with what they make, with the rank: Pair (A), Two pair (K,9), Three of a kind (7), Four of a kind (Q); each hand has one label, over the bottom of the cards only so the cards stay in view: on two boards it names both (Two pair (A,K) / Flush), on two lines where one would reach over the player's box, and run more than once it is the run being dealt, with its number (#2 Three of a kind (7)); once that hand is over the runs name their winners instead (on two boards a run, under each board). Once a hand is over, tapping your own cards shows them, one at a time, as well as the Show buttons. Chat appears in a bubble by the player's seat for a few seconds, and amounts show their cents. Card and chip sounds are recordings from Kenney's Casino Audio pack (CC0, kenney.nl; the license is in `public/sounds`): a card slid to each player as it is dealt, a card placed for each board card, cards shoved away for a fold, chips stacked for a call, a clink for a bet, a handful for a raise, a big push for an all-in, and the pot swept to the winner, each with a few takes picked at random. Every take is cut to start on its hit and kept short, most under a tenth of a second, and your own action sounds the moment you press it rather than when the table answers. The call when it is your turn, two knocks for a check and the host's request chime are made in the browser, and stand in for any recording that has not loaded yet. Browsers allow sound only after the first tap or key press on the page. The layout fits seats to the screen, from a phone held upright to a big monitor.

**On a computer.** As on PokerNow, there is no bar across the top: the table spreads across the window and everything else sits in its corners, sized with the window. Top left, The Aquarium (back to the club) and a menu down the side: Options (game options, ledger, stats, look and feel, the sealed deck), Hands, and, once you are seated, Away or I'm back, Add chips, Straddle (when the game has it), Call 30 (or the time left and Stay) and Leave seat. Top right, the game, the next game when they take turns (with the orbit), the blinds and the bomb pot count, with the 7-2 game, the straddle and the game clock under them, beside the sealed deck and a button that turns the sounds off and on. Bottom left, Log / Ledger (L) with the table's name beside it, the chat (M to write, the arrows to make it taller) and the voice box. Bottom right, the buttons.

**The action bar.** PokerNow's buttons in Apple's manner: Call and the amount (green), Raise or Bet (blue), Check (grey) and Fold (a red tint), filled and rounded, each with its key in the corner (C, R, K, F), and faint when they do nothing. Above them on your turn, Your turn with the seconds left, and Extra time +10s (T). Raise opens a frosted panel over the table with the amount, the bet sizes (min, half pot, three quarters, pot, and all in at no limit), a big slider with a thumb to grab and Raise to; Enter raises and Escape or Back closes it. For a seated player the buttons stay where they are all game, so the table never shifts as the turn moves round: when it is not your turn the same places hold pre-clicks while you are in the hand (Call and the amount or Call any, Check, Check/Fold or Fold), carried out the moment it is your turn and dropped once they no longer fit, or sit greyed out; each has a round tick that fills once picked. After a hand a panel shows your cards, all of them (Show all cards, S) or one at a time (1 to 5, each a small card of its own); on a phone it takes one row, where the actions go, so I'm back, Straddle, Call 30 and the rest stay in reach above it. While you are away, one big I'm back button takes their place. On a phone the buttons under the table give way to your turn in the same space, and the mute button comes along into the row. The run-it vote sits over the same space.

**Chat.** On a computer the chat sits bottom left, as on PokerNow: the latest messages, the balloon (or M) to write one, and Log / Ledger (or L) above it to open the log with the ledger over the right of the table. In a narrower window it is on the right, with Chat (a conversation, yours on the right) and Log (everything that happened, chat included) as two tabs, and Hide to close it; Chat in the top bar shows how many messages came in while it was closed. On a phone a chat button sits on the table with the same count, and opens the chat as a sheet from the bottom that keeps the table in view above it and stays above the keyboard. When someone adds chips, a line with a chip goes out in the chat for everyone ("Sam is adding 50.00 to their stack", with "after this hand" when they are in one, or "Sam rebuys for 100.00" when they had none left). Someone else's message comes in with a soft ping; the chips line always pings, while the other messages can be made silent with Chat sounds in Look and feel. The sound button in the top bar silences both.

**Look and feel.** Four colors by default (diamonds blue, clubs green, as on PokerNow) or two (red and black), large-index or classic card faces, felt color (the host sets the default, each player can override it), five card backs, the turn sound, the table sounds and the chat ping (each can be turned off), how to pick when everyone is all in, and a readout of what your hand makes. These are saved per browser. The font is Apple's system font where there is one, and Inter elsewhere; card numbers are Abril Fatface (Inter and Abril Fatface are under the SIL Open Font License and served from the app).

**Folded cards.** Three choices per game: hidden for good (the default), shown in the hand history after each hand, or shown when the game ends. With hidden for good, nobody ever sees another player's folded cards, and after a hand each player can show any of their cards, one at a time or all of them. The deal is still checked; see version 2 below.

**Downloads.** Every hand with its cards, results and actions, plus the full log and ledger as downloads.

**Voice chat.** Join voice (in the box beside the chat on a computer, in the bar at the bottom on a phone) asks for the microphone, then Mute and Unmute turn it off and on, and Leave voice ends it. Players and watchers can both join, up to 10 people per table. A green disc with a microphone beside a player's name shows they are in voice and can hear the table (a red one, crossed out, when muted), and it pulses while that person is talking; the bar says who else is in. The audio goes straight between browsers (WebRTC, with echo cancellation and noise suppression); the server only says who is in and passes on the messages that set up each connection, so it never hears anything. Joining on a second page or device takes over from the first, and after a reconnect the page joins again by itself. Most home and mobile connections reach each other directly through public STUN servers. Some networks block that; for them, set `TURN_URLS`, `TURN_USERNAME` and `TURN_CREDENTIAL` to a TURN relay (one on port 443 over TLS gets through most firewalls) and the audio goes through it.

**Calling 30.** Call 30 (in the menu down the left on a computer, in the bar at the bottom on a phone) tells the table a player is leaving in 30 minutes. A countdown shows on their seat for everyone and where Call 30 was, a pot won over 1,000 starts the 30 minutes again (no winning big and walking), and when it runs out they stand up after the hand in progress, their chips going back to their balance. Stay takes it back. Calling it is announced across the top of the table for everyone, with a chime and a clock that sweeps round, and so are a big pot starting it again and Stay. The countdown survives a server restart.

**Fair dealing page.** The club page, the sign-in page and the table's Sealed deck link to /fair, a page anyone can open without an account. It walks a player through checking a hand step by step: at the table (the seal for the next hand, the running count of hands the browser has checked, and Details for one hand), on their own computer with nothing from the site (Copy fingerprint before the deal, Download this hand, then `scripts/verify-hand.js` from a downloaded copy of the code), and a whole game in the verifier. It then explains how each hand is sealed and what the checks do and do not prove, and links the public code on GitHub along with the exact commit the site is running (Render's `RENDER_GIT_COMMIT`; `SOURCE_URL` points the link elsewhere).

**Leaving the page.** A seated player whose page closes (the tab is closed, they go to another page or the connection drops) and who is not back within 5 seconds checks when it costs nothing and folds otherwise, without their clock running down, and sits out from the next hand until they come back and press I'm back. Switching to another tab keeps the page connected, so it does not count, and a quick reload is not leaving.

**Any screen.** The table fits laptops, big monitors, tablets and phones held either way. On a computer the corners grow and shrink with the window; on a phone on its side the controls move to a column at the right so the table keeps its height.

## Proving the shuffle

Players who suspect the deal is rigged can check every hand. The scheme:

1. Before each hand the server picks a secret seed (32 random bytes) and publishes its SHA-256 fingerprint. The table shows it as a seal, a pattern drawn from the fingerprint, so players can compare it by eye.
2. After seeing that seal, each seated player's browser sends its own random input. The server committed to its seed before any input arrived, so it cannot choose a seed that favours anyone.
3. The deck is the standard 52-card order shuffled with Fisher-Yates, driven by HMAC-SHA256(seed, all inputs sorted alphabetically). Cards come off the top in order: hole cards one at a time starting left of the button, then the board, then any extra runs. No burn cards.
4. The seed is revealed after each hand, or when the game ends (host setting). Each player's browser then rebuilds the deck and checks the seal it saw before the deal, that its own input was used, its own hole cards and every board. A line appears in the log for each hand that passes.

Anyone can recheck any hand at `/verify`, which runs entirely in the browser, and can hash the revealed seed with any SHA-256 tool to compare it with the fingerprint. To leave the site out completely, download the hand from Details and check it from a copy of the code (GitHub's Download ZIP is enough, with Node.js 20 or newer):

```
node scripts/verify-hand.js hand-<table>-<number>.json --seal <fingerprint copied before the deal>
```

It runs the same checks with `shared/fairness.js` and Node's built-in cryptography, prints the rebuilt deal (folded cards that stay sealed show as ??), takes several files at once, and exits with 1 if any check fails.

What this proves: the deck was fixed before the deal, nothing was changed after the action started, every card came off that deck in order, and the players' own randomness was part of it. If the server dropped a player's input, that player's check fails.

A hand number only ever gets one seal. The seal for the next hand, and the inputs browsers have already sent for it, are saved with the table, so a restart (every deploy restarts the server) deals that hand from the seal everyone saw, and a browser whose input was lost in the restart sends the same input again. If the host changes how folded cards are kept, the next hand has to be sealed the other way: it takes the next number and the skipped number is noted in the log, so no browser sees two seals for one hand. A browser that saw one seal and was dealt from another would rightly report a failed check.

What it does not prove: that whoever runs the server cannot read cards from memory during a hand. That is true of PokerNow and any game dealt by a server. Closing that gap needs a "mental poker" protocol where no single party knows the deck, which is much slower and more complex.

Reveal timing is a trade-off. "After every hand" lets everyone check each hand immediately, but folded cards become visible in the hand history. "When the game ends" keeps folded cards private during play and checks everything at the end. The host can also reveal on demand.

**Version 2: folded cards hidden for good.** Revealing a seed shows every card, so games where folded cards stay hidden seal each card on its own instead:

1. Before the hand the server shuffles a base deck from its secret seed and seals every position i with SHA-256 of the position, the card and a secret 128-bit salt. The published seal is SHA-256 of all 52 card seals and the fingerprint of a secret key.
2. Players add their random inputs as before. HMAC-SHA256(key, all inputs) drives a Fisher-Yates shuffle of the 52 positions, so the server could not know where any card would end up when it sealed the deck.
3. After the hand the server reveals the key and opens (position, card, salt) for every card that was seen or never dealt: the boards, cards players showed, and the rest of the deck. Folded cards are published only as their seals. Each player's own cards are opened to that player alone.
4. Every browser rebuilds the published seal from the opened cards and the sealed ones, recomputes where each card went, and checks the boards, every shown card, its own cards and its own input. A card that was moved, swapped or renamed breaks the seal.

What version 2 gives up: nobody can check another player's folded cards, so a deck with a repeated card among folded hands could not be caught. The cards that were seen are all checked.

## Run it on your computer

You need Node.js 20 or newer (https://nodejs.org).

```
npm install
npm start
```

Open http://localhost:3000. People on the same Wi-Fi can join at `http://<your computer's local IP>:3000`.

## Put it online

The app is a single Node.js process that keeps live games in memory, so run exactly one instance. It needs WebSockets and a persistent disk for its data folder.

**Render.** Push this folder to a GitHub repository, then in Render choose New, Blueprint, and pick the repository. `render.yaml` creates a web service with a 1 GB disk mounted at `/data`. Use a paid instance: free instances sleep when idle and do not keep a disk, so accounts and ledgers would be lost.

**Docker** (Fly.io, Railway, or any server):

```
docker build -t the-aquarium .
docker run -p 3000:3000 -v aquarium-data:/data the-aquarium
```

Serve it over HTTPS (Render and Fly do this automatically). Settings come from environment variables:

| Variable | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PORT` | `3000` | Port to listen on |
| `DATA_DIR` | `./data` | Where accounts, clubs, ledgers, hand records and logs are stored |

**Loading fast.** On Render the deployed commit (`RENDER_GIT_COMMIT`) goes into every script and style address (`?v=`), with an import map so each module loads once, and browsers keep those files for a year; the pages themselves are checked on every visit, so a deploy shows at once. A page also asks for all of its modules at once instead of finding them import by import. Fonts and sounds are kept for a month. Without a commit (running locally) every file is checked on every visit, as before.

**Keeping going.** A mistake in one place is written to the log and the games carry on, rather than stopping the server. Every few seconds each running game is looked over: a hand that has stopped moving (no clock running for anyone and nothing about to happen, as after a step lost to an error), or a game that should be dealing and is not, is moved on, and the log says so. A finished game's hands are let go from memory 10 minutes after they were last opened, and read again from disk when someone opens them.

## Accounts and invites

The Aquarium is invite only. Nobody can see a table, a club or the lobby without an account, and an account can only be made from an invite link or approved from the join link.

- **The owner.** When the site has no owner, the server prints a one-time setup link in its log at every start (on Render: the service's Logs tab). Opening it creates the owner's account, or makes an existing account the owner.
- **Admins.** The owner makes and removes admins on the People page. Admins and the owner invite people by email (they join the club straight away), start games, look after the club ledger and send members password reset links. Only the owner changes admins or turns someone's access off (that signs them out everywhere; their balances stay as they are). Admins can remove members from the club (see Removing someone).
- **Invite links** work once, only for the email address they were sent to, and expire after 1 to 30 days. The invitee picks their own username and password. An admin can send a fresh link (the old one stops working) or cancel it.
- **The join link** is one reusable link to post in a group chat, for when invites are not emailed. Anyone who opens it picks a name, username and password (email optional) and asks to join; the request waits under Waiting to join on the club page and on the People page until an admin approves it (the account turns on and joins the club with a zero balance) or declines it (the account is removed, so the username is free again). Until then it cannot sign in. Admins make the link from Invite or the People page, can replace it (the old one stops working) or turn it off. Requests are limited to 5 per 10 minutes from one address and 50 waiting at once.
- **Roles from the club page.** The owner can open any member from Balances and switch them between Member and Admin.
- **Removing someone.** Remove from the club, on a member's sheet (open them from Balances) or on the People page, takes them out: they are signed out on every page at once, cannot sign in, and are no longer on the balances list; their past hands and ledger entries stay. A balance that is not zero is settled first, and the ledger says so (paid out to them, or their debt paid), after a confirmation that names the amount. Someone with chips on a table has to be taken off it first (Players, in the game's menu). Admins can remove members; only the owner can remove an admin, and nobody can remove the owner or themselves. Bring them back, on the People page, turns their access on and puts them back in the club with a zero balance; inviting their email again says to do that. Turning access off instead keeps them on the list with their balance.
- **Signed out elsewhere.** A page whose sign-in has ended (removed from the club, access turned off, a new password) goes to the sign-in page by itself.

**Sending the emails.** With these two settings the site emails invites and reset links from your Gmail:

| Variable | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `SMTP_USER` | your Gmail address |
| `SMTP_PASS` | a Gmail app password (Google Account, Security, 2-Step Verification on, then App passwords) |

Other providers also need `SMTP_HOST` and `SMTP_PORT`, and `MAIL_FROM` changes the sender name. Without them nothing is emailed: the admin gets the link to copy and send.

A forgotten owner password can also be reset on the server itself:

```
node scripts/reset-password.js <username> <new password>
```

**Moving to one club.** The first start of this version turns an older site into the single club: the owner's club (or the oldest one) becomes The Aquarium (renamed, which Club settings can change), every account becomes a member, and anyone who managed it becomes an admin. A club kept in whole chips moves to dollars with 1 chip = $1.00; games still running in chips are ended first, so everyone's chips go back to their balance. Older games keep showing the chips they were played with.

## Backups

Everything lives in the data folder: `db.json` (accounts, clubs, club ledgers, table settings and table ledgers), `hands/` (one file per table, every hand with its seed and cards) and `logs/`. Copy the folder to back it up. Once an hour the server keeps the last good `db.json` beside it as `db.json.bak`; if `db.json` cannot be read at start, the server starts from that copy and keeps the damaged file under another name. A line of a hands or log file left unfinished by a stop mid-write is skipped, and the next line starts on a line of its own. A save that fails (a full disk, say) is tried again with the next change. If the server restarts during a hand, that hand is cancelled and stacks go back to where they were when it started. Players who were playing are dealt in again as soon as their page reconnects (it does so on its own), as if they had pressed I'm back; anyone whose page is not back within a quarter of an hour stays away until they press it.

## Moving to a new server

The data folder can be handed from one running server to another, for example to a new Render address. Start the old server with `MIGRATION_EXPORT=on` and a long random `MIGRATION_KEY`, and the new one with `IMPORT_FROM=<old server URL>` and the same key. On its first boot, while its data folder is empty, the new server shows a short "moving in" page, copies every account, club, ledger, table and hand from the old one, then starts. From that moment the old server forwards every visitor to the new address and accepts no more changes. Logins carry over (players sign in once on the new address). On Render, `RENDER_EXTERNAL_URL` tells the old server where to send people; elsewhere, set it on the new server yourself. Remove `IMPORT_FROM` and the key once the move is done.

## Layout

```
server/        Express + Socket.io server
  engine/      one hand of poker (betting, pots, runs, showdown)
  table.js     seats, host controls, timers, ledger, commit and reveal
  clubs.js     the club: balances, credits, settlements, transfers
  stats.js     player stats from hand records
shared/        code used by both server and browser: cards, hand evaluator, fairness, money, replayer steps, all-in chances
public/        the web app: home (club balances, ledger and stats), start a game, table, verifier
scripts/       maintenance scripts, and verify-hand.js to check hands away from the site
test/          automated tests
```
